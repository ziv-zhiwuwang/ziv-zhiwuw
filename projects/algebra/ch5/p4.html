Exercise 5.3.

Let \( \alpha \) be the real positive fourth root of \(2\). Find all intermediate fields in the extension \( \mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \) of \( \mathbb{Q} \).

Proof.

One intermediate extension is

\[ \mathbb{Q} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}). \]

We claim that this is the only nontrivial intermediate extension. Suppose that we have an intermediate field

\[ \mathbb{Q} \hookrightarrow K \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}). \]

Then

\[ 4 = [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}) : \mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}) : K][K : \mathbb{Q}]. \]

If \(K\) is nontrivial and proper, then \([K : \mathbb{Q}] = 2\), and so \([\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}) : K] = 2\). Thus the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(K\) has degree \(2\). Since the roots of \(x^4 - 2\) are

\[ \alpha, \quad -\alpha, \quad i\alpha, \quad -i\alpha, \]

the possible quadratic factors containing \(x - \alpha\) are

\[ \begin{aligned} (x-\alpha)(x+\alpha) &= x^2 - \sqrt{2}, \\ (x-\alpha)(x-i\alpha) &= x^2 - (1+i)\alpha x + i\alpha^2, \\ (x-\alpha)(x+i\alpha) &= x^2 - (1-i)\alpha x - i\alpha^2. \end{aligned} \]

Since \(\mathbb{Q}(\alpha) \subseteq \mathbb{R}\), the last two cases cannot have coefficients in \(K\), because their coefficients are not real. Therefore the only possible quadratic minimal polynomial is

\[ x^2 - \sqrt{2}. \]

Hence \(\sqrt{2} \in K\), so \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) \subseteq K\). Since both fields have degree \(2\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\), we get

\[ K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}). \]

Therefore the intermediate fields are

\[ \mathbb{Q}, \qquad \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}), \qquad \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2}). \]

\(\square\)
